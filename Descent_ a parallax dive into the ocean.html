Dive in.

Scroll to sink. Every few hundred metres the light, the pressure and the residents all change.

Where the light still reaches.

Sunlight zone, to about 200 m

Enough sunlight gets through here for algae to grow, which is why most ocean life lives in this thin upper layer.

The light gives up.

Twilight zone, 200 to 1,000 m

Only a dim blue glow remains, far too weak for photosynthesis. Many animals here swim up at night to feed and sink back by day.

Make your own light.

Midnight zone, 1,000 to 4,000 m

Sunlight never arrives. Many deep-sea animals produce their own glow, called bioluminescence, to lure prey, hide from predators and signal each other.

Cold, dark and crushing.

Abyssal zone, 4,000 m and below

The pressure is hundreds of times what you feel at the surface, and the water sits close to freezing. Life here is slow, sparse and strange.
